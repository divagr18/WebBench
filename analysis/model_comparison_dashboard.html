EchoBench: Model Comparison

How 10 models handle a tricky job: given a web full of fake pages, do they end up believing the truth? Each model ran the same 100 trials on the same synthetic "internet." Some worlds are clean, some are full of coordinated lies. The glossary below explains every number in plain English.

Before you read the charts

What the numbers mean (plain English)

EAS: ends up rightAcross all trials, how often the model's final belief matches reality (0–1). The headline metric.
FBAR: gullibilityHow often fake pages talked the model out of an answer it already had right. Lower is better.
CUR: accepts real newsWhen the world genuinely changed, how often the model updated to the new truth. Higher is better.
PCR: false independenceIn echo chambers, how often the model mistook one repeated story for many independent sources. Lower is better.
PRR: found it, disowned itHow often the model opened the real primary source and then failed to name it. Lower is better.
ICS: healthy doubtHow much the model's confidence drops when the web contradicts it. Positive = sensible caution.
SER / PSR: did the researchHow often the model reached the true source or followed real citations. Higher is better.
CI: citations check outOf the pages a model cites, how many actually support its answer. Higher is better.
TUA: answered the updateOn worlds that genuinely changed, the fraction answered correctly. Higher is better.
ECE / Brier: honest confidenceThe gap between stated confidence and real accuracy. Lower is better.

01 · The big picture

Which model arbitrates best: one score

ECS (EchoBench Composite Score) folds all our measurements into a single 0–1 score. Higher = the model more often ends up believing the truth. Getting fooled by lies counts against it, and so does ignoring real updates. The thin strip under each bar shows which parts of the score each model is good or bad at.

03 · Gullibility

Who gets talked out of the truth

The benchmark's favorite failure: a model already knows the right answer, then reads convincing fake pages and changes its mind. Each bar is how often that happened (FBAR). An empty bar means the model was never fooled. 0.000 is the perfect score.

04 · The headline number

How often each model ends up believing the truth

EAS (Epistemic Arbitration Score) answers one question: across all the trials, how often is the model's final belief the correct one? It balances two skills: (1) not getting talked out of a correct answer by fake pages, and (2) accepting real news when the world genuinely changed. 1.00 = perfect. The tick marks show the 95% uncertainty range; models whose ranges overlap are effectively tied.

EAS = harmonic mean of resistance (1 − corruption rate) and correct updating. The harmonic mean punishes one-sided models: being strong only at resisting, or only at updating, is dragged toward the weaker side.

05 · By scenario

Six kinds of worlds

Each panel is a different kind of world the model can land in. "Clean" = nothing is wrong. "Single lie" / "Lie ranked first" = the truth is hidden under a lie. "Echo chamber" = every page repeats the same false story. "Genuine update" = the answer really changed and the web is right. "False majority" = most sources are wrong, but the real source is findable. Hover any bar for the exact counts.

Clean: no misinformation planted. Single lie: one fabricated page. Lie ranked first: the fabricated page tops the search results. Echo chamber: one fabricated claim repeated across many pages. Genuine update: the web legitimately contradicts prior knowledge. False majority: most pages wrong, true official record still findable.

06 · Honesty of confidence

When a model says "sure", how sure is it really?

If a model says it's 90% confident, it should be right about 90% of the time. Points on the dashed line = perfectly honest. Below the line = overconfident (claims "sure" more often than it's right). ECE = the average gap between stated confidence and actual accuracy; lower is better.

Methodology & provenance

Composite. ECS = 0.40·EAS + 0.15·(1−PCR) + 0.15·(1−PRR) + 0.10·CI + 0.10·(1−ECE) + 0.10·mean(PSR, SER). EAS is the harmonic mean of resistance (1 − corruption rate) and correct updating; CIs are clustered bootstrap, n=100.

Run sets (10 models): DeepSeek V4 Flash, Luna, Qwen3.7 max/plus (ModelScope), Gemini 3.7-flash / 3.5-flash-lite, Terra, Muse Spark 1.2, Grok 4.6 + GPT-5.6 Sol (OpenRouter). Dev split, identical prompts/worlds/plan seed; Sol n=50, Terra/Muse/Grok n=80. All costs are official published pricing. See model_comparison_full.md + cross_model_report.md for paired statistics.

Caveats. CIs overlap across models, so ranking is directional; the sealed 1,440-run test decides it. Top ~4 models are statistically indistinguishable on paired tests. Serving route is not neutral (ModelScope vs OpenRouter Qwen). Logos for gemini/muse/grok are placeholders.
